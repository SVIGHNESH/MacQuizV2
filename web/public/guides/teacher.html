Walkthrough № MQ-T1 For the teacher

From a blank sheet to a settled result.

Everything you do on MacQuiz is one of four moves: set the paper, choose who sits it, invigilate the hall while it runs, and settle the marks afterwards. This walkthrough goes through all four in the order you will actually meet them.

Section A - Setting the paper

First you write the questions, one way or the other.

A new quiz starts as a Draft. Nothing about a Draft is visible to a single student, so you can leave one half-written for a week without consequence.

  1. Q1Sign in

    Sign in with the account you were given

    There is no sign-up page anywhere in MacQuiz, by design. Your admin creates every teacher and student account. On your first sign-in you will be asked to replace the temporary password before you can go anywhere else.

  2. Q2Author

    Create the quiz and add questions

    Write questions one at a time in the editor. It autosaves as you type, so a half-written question is never lost. Each question needs its correct answer present among the options and points above zero; choice questions take two to eight options. Drag to reorder.

    Optionally tag a question with a topic. Tags are what later turn into each student's strength-and-weakness breakdown, so a little discipline here pays off at analytics time.

  3. Q3Or bulk import

    Or upload the whole paper as a file

    Download the CSV template, fill in one row per question (type, question, option_a…option_f, correct, points, plus an optional topic column), and upload it. Up to 500 rows and 10 MB.

    • The file is validated before a single question is written.
    • Errors come back per row and column, with a downloadable report.
    • Fix and re-upload as many times as you like; nothing is committed until it is clean.
    • A clean file commits all-or-nothing. Imported questions are ordinary questions afterwards.
  4. Q4Guardrails

    Decide how tightly the hall is watched

    Guardrails are set per quiz, before you publish, and are frozen into the paper at that moment. Each one can be off, warn-only, or counted toward the violation ladder.

    Fullscreen lock

    Off / warn / count

    The player goes fullscreen at start; leaving raises a violation and overlays a blocker until they return.

    Focus tracking

    Off / warn / count

    Tab switches and window blur are reported with their duration, so you see "left the tab for 40 s" rather than a bare flag.

    Clipboard & right-click

    Off / on

    Copy, cut, paste and the context menu are disabled in the player, and attempts to use them are logged.

    Single active session

    Always on

    Starting on a second device invalidates the first. Not configurable, and always recorded.

    The violation ladder After the threshold you set (three counted violations by default) one action fires: flag just marks the roster row, notify alerts you and lets the student carry on, and auto-submit ends the attempt. Flag is the default on purpose - browser guardrails deter and document, they do not prove intent, so the decision to act stays with you.
Section B - Choosing the hall

Then you decide who sits it, and exactly when.

Publishing is the moment a Draft becomes real: the questions and guardrails are snapshotted into a version, and the quiz appears on the dashboards of the students you picked.

  1. Q5Audience

    Assign the students

    Pick students individually or by group. This is not a filter on a list everyone can see - a student who is not assigned cannot see the quiz at all, and an attempt to reach it directly returns a plain "not found".

  2. Q6Window

    Set the window and the duration

    Three separate controls, and the difference matters:

    • 1Start time - the earliest anyone can begin. Before it, students see a countdown.
    • 2End time - the hard close. No new starts, and every attempt still open is submitted.
    • 3Duration - each student's personal budget, which starts when they start.

    A student who starts late gets whichever is smaller: their full duration, or the time left until the window closes. That deadline is computed once, on the server, at the moment they begin.

  3. Q7Publish

    Publish, and know what you can still change

    Publishing needs at least one question, at least one assigned student, and a valid future window. The quiz becomes Scheduled and opens by itself at the start time - you do not need to be online for it.

    While it is still Scheduled you can reschedule it, or cancel it back to Draft to keep editing. Once the start time passes, cancelling is refused; from then on the way out is to close it.

The state of a paper
Draft

Yours alone. Invisible to students, freely editable.

Scheduled

Published and waiting. Reschedule or cancel until the start time.

Live

Students can sit it. Extend, close early, kick, or invite late.

Closed

Open attempts force-submitted, grading runs, results settle.

Archived

Read-only. The analytics stay.

Section C - Invigilating

While it runs, you are standing in the hall.

The live monitor is the closest thing MacQuiz has to walking between desks. It updates as things happen, not on a refresh.

  1. Q8Watch

    Read the live roster Live

    One row per assigned student, showing who has started, which question they are on, how many answers are in, who has submitted, and who has dropped off the network. Amber badges carry violation counts; hover for the breakdown.

  2. Q9Intervene

    Extend, invite, close early

    • Extend the end time when the hall lost ten minutes to a power cut.
    • Add a student late - they get whatever time is left.
    • Force-close to end the paper early for everyone at once.

    Removing a student from the audience is refused while they have an attempt in progress. Interrupting someone mid-paper has exactly one sanctioned route, and it is the next step.

  3. Q10Remove

    Kick a candidate, on the record

    Two deliberate steps: pick a reason from the list or write your own, then confirm. The attempt ends on the server the instant you confirm - not when the student's browser notices - and the student sees a screen carrying your reason.

    What a kick does not do It does not discard their work. Whatever was autosaved is still graded, flagged as kicked wherever the score appears, and you can override that score to zero. If it was a mistake, re-admit them: they get one fresh attempt with the time that remains, and the kicked attempt stays in the record. Nothing here is ever quietly erased.
Section D - Settling the marks

Afterwards, the paper tells you what it found.

Grading starts the moment the quiz closes. You see scores as they land; students see nothing until you release them.

  1. Q11Release

    Release the results when you are ready

    If you chose automatic release at publish time, scores and the answer key reach students as soon as grading finishes. If you chose manual, they wait - useful when you want to moderate a few scores or hold a re-sit first. Releasing is one button, and it is recorded.

  2. Q12Read

    Read the item analysis, not just the average

    Per quiz you get the score distribution, mean and median, participation, and an integrity summary. The useful part is per question:

    • Difficulty - what share of the class got it right.
    • Discrimination - whether it separated strong students from weak ones. A near-zero value on a hard question usually means the question is broken, not the class.
    • Option-pick rates - which wrong answer was tempting, and why.
    • Average time spent on the question.
  3. Q13Follow up

    Look along a student, not just across a paper

    Per student you get their score and percentile, their accuracy trend across every quiz you have set them, average time per question, how often they actually sat what they were assigned, and - if you tagged your questions - their accuracy per topic. Export any of it as CSV.

    Topic accuracy only counts questions the student actually answered, so a skipped question is never held against them as a weakness.

Three things worth remembering

The rules that settle every argument.

The clock is ours
Every deadline is the server's. A student's laptop clock, a paused tab, or a dead battery changes nothing about when their time ran out.
Answers are saved as they are typed
Not at submit. A student whose browser dies mid-paper has lost seconds of work, not the paper - and their attempt is auto-submitted at the deadline regardless.
Nothing is quietly deleted
Kicks, re-admissions, releases, extensions and edits are all recorded with who did them and when. The audit log is append-only.
